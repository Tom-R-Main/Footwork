Kestrel Bay Lighthouse

The Kestrel Bay Lighthouse stands on the eastern headland of Kestrel Bay. It was first lit in 1873 after three winters of construction, and its light could be seen from 19 nautical miles at sea.

The tower is 41 metres tall and built from local granite. Its first keeper, Margaret Ilse Hale, served for 31 years and kept the logbooks that the museum displays today.

The lamp was converted from oil to electricity in 1921 and automated in 1968. The station was decommissioned in 1994 and reopened as a museum in 2001.
