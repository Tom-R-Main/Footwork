# Q6. Does the calibration survive contact with live sites?

Status: pre-registered 2026-09-21; task set authored; not run.

## Observation

Every number in `results/` was measured on one local fixture site. On it, Jev's choice confidence
never bound (about 1.0 on nearly every step); the situation nouls (`stuck`, `destructive`) did the
arbitration work, and verification did the rest. Benchmarks have overstated web-agent progress
before [V14]; online evaluation with key intermediate states is the corrective [V11].

## Hypothesis

On live pages the S1 escalation rate is at least double the fixture site's, and choice confidence
stops saturating: it takes values below 0.95 on at least a quarter of steps and is informative
about step correctness.

## Predictions

If true: escalation rate on `live-dev` ≥ 2× the dev fixture rate (dev fixture, `dev-dual-v3`:
55 S2 of 95 steps, but most were reads; the comparable figure is escalations from S1-eligible
steps, computed the same way on both); ≥ 25% of S1 steps have op confidence < 0.95; reliability
diagram shows slope > 0.

If false: confidence stays ≥ 0.95 on ≥ 90% of steps, or the escalation rate is within 1.5× of the
fixture rate.

## Amendment, 2026-09-21 13:10, written before the live result was read

`evals/metrics.py` on the fixture baseline (`results/dev-dual-v3-20260921-043905`, dual arm):
95 S1-eligible steps, 55 escalated (58%), but 47 of those are verification vetoes of S1's own
`done` and 5 are the destructive gate; only 3 are confidence floors. 28% of steps already have op
confidence below 0.95 on the fixture, so the original "≥ 25%" prediction cannot discriminate.
Sharpened predictions, same hypothesis:

- If true: confidence-floor escalations (operation or target confidence) are at least 2× the
  fixture rate (3 of 95, 3.2%), so ≥ 6.3% of S1-eligible steps; and the fraction of S1-eligible
  steps with op confidence below 0.95 is at least 1.5× the fixture's 28%, so ≥ 42%.
- If false: confidence-floor escalations ≤ 3.2%, or the below-0.95 fraction ≤ 28%.
- Verification vetoes and destructive pauses are reported but excluded from the escalation
  comparison, since they measure the verifier and the gate, not the arbitration signal.

## Amendment 2, 2026-09-21 14:05, after validation run 0 (`results/q6-live-validation.md`)

Repeats reduced from three per arm to one (`live-dev-r1`, all three arms, judge verdicts recorded)
plus one paired rerun of all three arms after the trajectory ledger lands. Reason: run 0 already
fixed the direction of the hypothesis and showed the verifier, not arbitration, is the cost driver;
further repeats of the current verifier would measure a configuration we intend to replace. The
paired rerun after the ledger is the real measurement; intervals come from the paired task-level
differences of the two runs, and any claim beyond direction says so.

## Design

Arms: `s1_only`, `stock`, `dual`, frozen settings (`results/e1-frozen.md`), three repeats per arm
on `live-dev.yaml`. Max 25 steps. Same System 2 (Muse Spark 1.3 Contributor). Live heldout is run
once per arm after dev, never debugged.

Primary metric: escalation rate from S1-eligible steps, and expected calibration error of op
confidence against step correctness (labelled from outcomes: a step is wrong if the run later
reverts it, loops, or ends failed without it contributing to a checkpoint; exact rule in the
metrics module before labelling).

Guardrails: crash rate per arm; false completions; unreachable tasks (no arm passes in any repeat)
reported and excluded from paired comparisons.

## Decision rule

- Confidence miscalibrated but informative (ECE > 0.1, AUROC > 0.6): Q1 sets thresholds from ROC.
- Confidence calibrated and never binding (≥ 90% of steps ≥ 0.95): drop the confidence floors from
  the arbiter; the nouls stay.
- Escalation rate not elevated: the fixture site was representative for this arbiter, and Q2/Q5
  proceed on the fixture and live sets together.
