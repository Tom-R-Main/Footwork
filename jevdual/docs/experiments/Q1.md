# Q1. What signal should trigger deliberation?

Status: pre-registered 2026-09-21. Depends on Q6 traces and step labels.

## Observation

On the fixture site Jev's choice confidence saturated while the situation nouls did the work:
`stuck` flagged the form loops and `destructive` stopped the delete. The literature frames
arbitration as a meta-reasoning policy conditioned on the fast policy's uncertainty [A1] with a
separate metacognitive module choosing the solver [A4][A8], and offers a measurement:
metacognitive sensitivity, whether a confidence signal separates correct from incorrect actions
(meta-d′, ROC) [A5].

## Hypothesis

Calibrated situation nouls predict a wrong S1 step with AUROC at least 0.10 higher than choice
confidence, because confidence saturates on easy menus.

## Predictions

If true: AUROC(best noul or the effects diff) − AUROC(op confidence) ≥ 0.10 on the labelled steps,
with the bootstrap interval excluding 0.

If false: op confidence matches or beats every noul, or no signal reaches AUROC 0.7.

## Design

Offline over the Q6 traces. Signals per S1 step: op confidence, target confidence, each noul
(`goal_done`, `stuck`, `needs_reasoning`, `destructive`), the effects diff. Labels: roughly 300
steps hand-labelled right or wrong, stratified to include S1-only's confident wrong dones.
Compute ROC, AUROC, meta-d′ per signal; paired bootstrap on AUROC differences. `evals/sweep.py`
already replays traces; the metrics module adds the curves.

Primary metric: AUROC difference. Guardrail: labels are made before any curve is looked at.

## Decision rule

- Supported: arbiter thresholds are set from the best signal's ROC at a pre-chosen operating point
  (false-escalation rate ≤ 20%), replacing the hand-set numbers in `arbiter.toml`.
- Falsified with confidence winning: keep the confidence floors and drop nouls that add nothing.
- No signal reaches 0.7: the arbiter needs a new signal (a verification-style noul on the proposed
  action's precondition), not new numbers.

### Preliminary look, 2026-09-21, heuristic labels only (not the pre-registered test)

`evals.metrics --labels` on `results/live-dev-v0-20260921-110328/labels.csv`: 102 S1-acted steps
with heuristic labels (17 wrong), no human labels yet. AUROC for predicting a wrong step: op
confidence 0.53, target confidence 0.61, `needs_reasoning` 0.61, `stuck` 0.78, `goal_done` 0.28
(high `goal_done` goes with right steps), `destructive` 0.50. Two caveats that make this a look,
not evidence: the heuristic marks a step wrong partly from the *next* step's `stuck` verdict, which
inflates `stuck`'s score; and acted steps are range-restricted on confidence (they already passed
the floors), which deflates the confidence scores. The escalated steps (224) have no label at all
without a person. Human labels on the sheet are the next input.

## Amendment, 2026-09-23: labels are model-produced under a blinded two-pass protocol, human-audited

The 530-row annotation set (`results/annotation/q1-s1-decisions.csv`, from the three runs whose traces
carry menus and scores) was labelled by two independent Claude Fable passes, each blinded to the
executed action, the next step's reason and the run outcome (packets built by
`scripts/build_label_packets.py`; rubric `results/annotation/RUBRIC.md`). The label used is the one
both passes gave; disagreements (17) and `unclear` (22) are excluded from the curves. A 40-row audit
sheet (20 disagreements or heuristic conflicts, 20 random) goes to the human; agreement (kappa) with
the model labels is reported beside the result, and below 0.7 the labels are demoted to "assisted"
and more are hand-labelled. Muse is System 2 and Jev is System 1, so the labeller is independent of
both. Pass-to-pass agreement: 96.8%, kappa 0.91. The heuristic `auto_label` agrees with the model
labels at kappa 0.23 on the acted steps and is retired as a reference.

## Result (`results/annotation/q1-calibration.md`, 2026-09-23, pending the human audit)

Mixed, and the useful finding is structural. Over all 491 labelled decisions (81 wrong), op
confidence predicts a wrong step at AUROC 0.59 [0.52, 0.66]; target confidence 0.66 [0.60, 0.73];
the best noul (destructive) 0.64; goal_done is anti-predictive (0.35). No signal reaches 0.7 overall,
and no noul beats op confidence by the pre-registered 0.10 with an interval clear of zero. On the
steps System 1 acted on (238, 43 wrong), needs_reasoning (0.69, +0.15 [+0.07, +0.24] over op
confidence) and target confidence (0.67, +0.14 [+0.01, +0.25]) do clear it.

By operation the picture splits:

- `click` (134, 24 wrong): target confidence AUROC 0.80 [0.69, 0.90]. The current floor 0.45 catches
  4% of wrong clicks; a floor of 0.70 catches 54% at 12% false escalation, 0.80 catches 71% at 18%.
  The decision rule's "supported" branch applies: the click floor is set from this ROC.
- `type` (225, 35 wrong, the largest wrong class at 25%): nothing predicts it (op 0.50, target 0.58,
  best noul 0.63 with intervals crossing 0.5). The wrong typing steps are value errors, not target
  errors: a product name typed into Username, a password literal into Username, a username as the
  password. System 1's confidences are about the target; the value comes from code
  (`literal_from_task`, known values). This is the rule's third branch: the arbiter needs a new
  signal, a noul on whether the value belongs in the field, asked before typing.
- `done` (78, 5 wrong): goal_done AUROC 0.92; the verifier already gates every done.

Decision, held until the audit confirms the labels: per-operation target floors (click raised toward
0.70 to 0.80 from the ROC; type unchanged), a value-fit noul on typing steps, goal_done dropped from
the escalation rules for anything but done. Each change is measured on live-dev before it is frozen
in `arbiter.toml`.
