# Q15. Does a footwork session run alongside a person without taking their work, input or focus?

Pre-registered 2026-09-25, before any measured run of the suite. Build: the commits that add
`jevdual.posture` (execution posture enforced at dispatch), `jevdual.ax` (exact append), the
target-identity changes in `jevdual.native` (ba4c0f9, 0a242ad) and the suite itself
(`jevdual.coexist`, `tests/live/test_coexistence.py`, `scripts/q15_coexistence.py`, cited in the
commit that adds this file). Plan: `docs/plans/legible-harness.md`, "Running alongside a person".

## Observation

Codex's background computer use and the Claude Code review of it (2026-09-25) named the difference:
an agent that addresses a tab or an element can act without the person's foreground, but addressing
alone does not make a workflow safe to run beside someone. Reading footwork at bbba8d5 found four
concrete gaps:

- **Silent foreground escalation.** A key the Driver refused for `same_pid_keyboard_ambiguity` was
  resent with foreground delivery, and the fallback was only recorded afterwards.
- **Data rebuilt from a preview.** `append` wrote back the model's 480-character, whitespace-collapsed
  preview plus the new line. Fed 1,000 A's, it wrote back 479 A's and an ellipsis. The Driver also
  strips trailing whitespace from values (measured).
- **Compound steps with no target on one half.** `enter` did an unchecked focusing click and then a
  key; `scroll` went to a screen point with no target; `hover` moved the pointer.
- **Menus leave the window in front.** `menu` fronted the window (`bring_to_front` leaves it there) and
  never handed the front back.

## Hypothesis

H1: with the posture, the exact append and the identity changes, a session in `background_only`
completes its steps on its own target while a person types in another app, in another document of
the same process, or in another tab of the same browser window, with the person's work exactly as
they typed it and their focus never taken. In `foreground_permitted` it takes the front only after
the person has been idle for 3 s, gives the front back afterwards, and never overrides a move the
person made during the step.

H0: any case below fails one of its checks in any of three repetitions.

## The promises and their cases

Each case is judged on four conditions together:
1. **Task end state:** exactly right, read through accessibility or the page's own report, never
   through the Driver that acted.
2. **Person's work:** exactly what the scripted person sent, in their window's own record.
3. **Target identity:** nothing written anywhere else.
4. **No prohibited side effects:** the front app, the key window and the pointer are unchanged, and
   no keystroke was held back because the person's window lost the front.

| case | setup | the session does | checks |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | person types in their own app (Tk); session on a one-window TextEdit document, `background_only` | append, key `x`, scroll, append, hotkey `cmd+s` | document == original + line + `x` + line exactly; person's text exact, nothing held; front never left the person; the hotkey not sent (`requires_foreground`); every sent step background; the agent never moved the pointer |
| C2 | person types in document A; session on document B of the **same** TextEdit process, `background_only` | append, key `x`, append | B exact; A == person's text exactly; the key not sent (`requires_foreground`, the ambiguity); A stays the key window; front never left the person |
| C3 | person types in tab A of a Chrome window; session in tab B of the **same window** through the Driver's browser mode | type a name, click Submit | the page records exactly one submit with the typed name; tab A's text == person's text; tab A never hidden or blurred; tab B never shown; front never left the person |
| C4 | person in their app; session on a two-window TextEdit (keys need the foreground), `foreground_permitted` | (a) a chord while the person types; (b) a foreground chord after 3.6 s idle, then the person types again; (c) a menu step during which the person moves to a second window of theirs | (a) not sent, `human_active`; (b) sent, foreground, the front is the person's afterwards, their text exact across both bursts; (c) after the person's move lands, neither the agent's app nor their previous window takes the front again (amended, see below); their text there exact; both documents unchanged |
| C5 | person types in their app; a 9,030-character document with tabs, double spaces, astral characters and trailing whitespace; another actor inserts a line at the start between the session's look and its append | append (refused), look, append | the first append not sent (`precondition_changed`) and the document exactly the other actor's version; the second append exact over the whole document; person's text exact; front never left the person |

## Measured limit, reported not judged

In `foreground_permitted`, the person's keystrokes typed while a foreground step holds the front
land in the agent's window. The idle gate makes this unlikely and the menu route now waits for
activation instead of a fixed 0.4 s. C4 records how long our window held the front (`fronts`); the
result reports the longest interval. `background_only` has no such interval, which is why it is
the operator's default.

## Design

- Three repetitions of C1 to C5, in that order, each in fresh processes (a new TextEdit instance
  with `open -n -g` and window restoration off, fresh Tk surfaces, a fresh Chrome profile on its own
  debugging port). Command:
  `FOOTWORK_LIVE=1 uv run python scripts/q15_coexistence.py --reps 3 --out results/q15-coexistence`.
- The scripted person types through the HID event tap at one character per 60 ms. Before each
  keystroke they check that their own app is in front and hold the key when it is not.
- **Contamination, not failure.** The suite starts only after 20 s without input. A case is marked
  `contaminated` and rerun, not counted, when either:
  - the pointer moved outside every agent step, or
  - the HID key-down counter rose by more than the keystrokes the suite sent.

  A pointer move inside an agent step is the agent's failure.
- Smoke runs before the measured run are allowed to find harness bugs. Any change to a case after the
  first measured repetition is an amendment, dated below.

## Primary metric and decision rule

Primary: the cases passed of 15 (5 cases × 3 repetitions), with every check in a case required.

- **All 15 pass:** each promise in the table becomes a row in the plan's conformance table, backed by
  its case and its unit test (`tests/test_posture.py`), and footwork claims "runs alongside you" for
  `background_only` (C1, C2, C3, C5) and for `foreground_permitted` as C4 specifies.
- **A case fails:** its promise is withdrawn or fixed, and the fix is re-run in three new
  repetitions. A failure is never averaged away.

## Guardrails

- No write to any file or window outside the suite's own (the suite owns every process it touches
  and kills them at the end).
- The existing unit suite stays green.

## Amendments

- **2026-09-25, before the first measured repetition, from smoke runs 2 to 4.**
  - **Harness faults fixed.**
    - TextEdit auto-capitalises the person's first letter, so their text now starts with a capital.
    - Chrome selects the first URL's tab, so C3 opens the person's tab first and refuses to start
      unless it is selected.
    - The person's window move went through the agent's own Driver and queued behind its calls; it
      now uses an exact activation of its own.
  - **Bridge faults found and fixed.**
    - The Driver's `bring_to_front` resolves by bundle and activated the wrong one of two Python
      processes. The hand-back now activates by exact pid.
    - The Driver's `invoke_menu` re-activated the agent's app 1.9 s after the person had moved away
      and held the front for 846 ms. The menu route now fronts the bound window, presses the item
      through AX (which never re-activates the app) and hands the front back to the person's latest
      choice; our window held the front for about 80 ms in a probe.
    - `browser_type` selects a background tab for about 100 ms except as an insertion into an empty
      field. footwork-46 made replacing held text a posture-governed foreground step (868f258).
  - **C4(c) check made precise.** "Nothing is handed back" is stricter than the promise: a hand-back to
    the person's previous window that lands before their move overrides nothing. The check is now that
    after the person's move lands, neither the agent's app nor their previous window takes the front.
- **2026-09-25, after the measured run.** C3's agent steps were not wrapped in the per-step pointer
  read that C1, C2, C4 and C5 use. A pointer move caused by the Driver's browser calls would therefore
  have been filed as contamination rather than failing the case. They are now wrapped, and C3 was run
  three more times (`results/q15-coexistence-c3-attributed/`).

## Result

Run 2026-09-25 at 2440532 (`results/q15-coexistence/`: `cases.jsonl` has one row per attempt,
`summary.md`). **15 of 15 cases passed, every check in each.** One attempt was contaminated and
rerun after the full idle guard: rep 2, C3, the pointer moved 33 px outside any C3 step. The three
attributed C3 repetitions (at 2440532 plus the wrapping) passed 3 of 3, with no pointer move inside
any agent step.

What the rows show beyond pass or fail:
- **C1: sent steps stay in the background.** Every sent step was background: two appends, a key and
  a scroll. The hotkey was not sent (`requires_foreground`). The document read back exactly (3,010
  characters kept) with the background key `x` landing after the first appended line. The person's
  61 characters were exact, and the front never left their app.
- **C2: the ambiguity is refused, not escalated.** The key to document B was not sent
  (`requires_foreground`, the same-process ambiguity). Document A, which the person was typing in,
  stayed the key window and received exactly their text. Both of the session's appends to B landed
  exactly.
- **C3: background tab.** The agent's tab received one page-level `focus` event while hidden and
  was never shown. The person's tab was never hidden or blurred. The single submit carried exactly
  "Ada Lovelace", and the person's tab held exactly their text.
- **C4: foreground_permitted behaves as registered.**
  - (a) The step yielded (`human_active`) while the person typed.
  - (b) It ran after 3.6 s idle, the front was back with the person, and both of their bursts
    arrived exactly.
  - (c) The menu was pressed through AX and handed back. Nothing retook the front after the person
    moved.
- **C5: conflict refused, then exact.** The append was refused (`precondition_changed`) after
  another actor's edit, leaving the document exactly as they left it. The second append was exact
  over 9,030 characters with astral characters and trailing whitespace.

**Measured limit** (`foreground_permitted`): the agent's window held the front for 36 to 65 ms per
foreground step, the longest being 65 ms. Front samples are about 30 ms apart, so a shorter hold can
be missed. The person's move overlapped a step in 0 of 3 repetitions: the step is over before a Tk
window finishes activating (about 1 s). The move-during-step rule, "hand back to their latest
choice", is therefore backed by `test_menu_hands_the_front_back_but_never_fights_the_person`, not
by a live overlap.

**Decision.** Adopted as registered. footwork claims "runs alongside you" for `background_only`
(C1, C2, C3, C5) and, for `foreground_permitted`, exactly what C4 shows: yield while the person
types, take the front for tens of milliseconds only after 3 s idle, hand it back, never retake it
after the person moves. Each promise is a row in `docs/plans/legible-harness.md` with its unit test
and its case.

**Not covered** (stated, not claimed):
- **Apps outside the suite.** Electron, Catalyst and web views in native apps are untested.
- **Chrome's accessibility route.** The operator's Chrome session uses the browser mode (C3) and
  refuses background keys to a multi-window Chrome.
- **A person typing into the agent's own document.** The contract excludes it: a whole-field edit
  needs ownership.
- **Races faster than the 20 ms front sampling.**
